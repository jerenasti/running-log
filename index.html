Garmin heart rate data needed

Training load and aerobic efficiency are calculated from the time you spend in each heart rate zone, which comes from Garmin. Fetch your data with start.bat. Activities recorded without a heart rate can't be used.

Training load

Fitness, fatigue and form from your daily heart rate load.

How it's calculated

Daily load (Edwards TRIMP): minutes in heart rate zone 1 × 1 + zone 2 × 2 + … + zone 5 × 5, summed over the day's activities. An hour in zone 2 gives 120; an hour in zone 4 gives 240.

Fitness (CTL): 42-day weighted average of daily load. Each day moves it 1/42 of the way toward that day's load, so it changes slowly and shows what your body is used to.

Fatigue (ATL): the same with 7 days. It reacts quickly to recent hard days.

Form (TSB): yesterday's fitness − yesterday's fatigue. Negative means you are carrying fatigue from training (normal during a build); positive means you are fresh, e.g. before a race.

Acute:chronic workload ratio

Last 7 days compared with the last 28.

Indicative
How it's calculated

ACWR = average daily load of the last 7 days ÷ average daily load of the last 28 days. Around 1.0 means you are training about as much as you are used to. A quick rise above 1.3–1.5 has been linked to a higher injury risk, but the research is mixed, so use it as a rough guide, not a rule.

Aerobic efficiency

Easy runs only. Higher is better.

No easy runs found yet.

How it's calculated

Efficiency = average speed in metres per minute ÷ average heart rate. Running 6:00 /km (167 m/min) at 140 bpm gives 1.19. If it rises over time, you run faster at the same heart rate: your aerobic fitness is improving. The line is the average of the last 5 easy runs, which smooths out day-to-day noise such as heat or hills.

Easy run: a run of at least 30 minutes with heart rate data, no interval, tempo, repeats, threshold, rolling, progressive, fartlek or race in the name, named easy or recovery or classified by Garmin as Recovery or Aerobic base, and an average heart rate below zone 4.

No recovery data yet

HRV, sleep and Body Battery come from Garmin. Fetch your data with start.bat. Your watch needs to be worn overnight and synced.

How the recommendation works

Rest day: HRV is below your normal range and resting HR is above it on the same morning, or either of them is off while your training load is high.

Easy day: any one recovery signal is off (low HRV, high resting HR, poor sleep, low Body Battery), or your training load is high.

Hard training OK: none of the above.

High training load: workload ratio (ACWR) 1.3 or higher, or fatigue (ATL) above its own normal range (28-day mean + 1 standard deviation).

The recommendation uses the latest night your watch has synced. It is a rule of thumb from your own data, not medical advice: if you feel ill or unusually tired, rest anyway.

HRV, last 4 weeks

Nightly average and Garmin's normal range for you.

What is this?

Heart rate variability (HRV) is the variation in time between heartbeats, measured by your watch while you sleep. Within or above your normal range usually means you have recovered. A drop below it can be a sign of fatigue, illness or stress.

The shaded band is Garmin's baseline for you, based on the last 3 weeks. Nights below it are highlighted. The dashed line is the 7-day average.

Resting heart rate, last 4 weeks

Daily value and your normal range.

What is this?

Resting heart rate is your lowest heart rate of the day. A value above your normal range can point to fatigue, illness, heat, stress or alcohol.

The shaded band is your normal range: the mean ± 1 standard deviation of the 28 days before each date, so about two thirds of your days fall inside it. It appears once there are 14 days of data. Days above it are highlighted.

Last night's sleep

What is this?

Sleep stages as measured by your watch. Deep sleep matters most for physical recovery, REM for mental recovery. The sleep score (0–100) is Garmin's overall rating of duration, stages and restlessness.
